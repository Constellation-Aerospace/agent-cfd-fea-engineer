Stratospheric Payload Transient Thermal Analysis: 0-40 km, 160 min, 30 W, Cu-ETP Straps

Study: balloon-ttp/transient-0-40km-etp-copper · Date: 2026-08-06 · Prepared by: agent-cfd-fea-engineer

1. Executive Summary

This study re-solves the completed 0→40 km / 160-minute / 30 W ascent transient (balloon-ttp/transient-0-40km) with the twin thermal-strap braids changed from OFHC copper (κ 390 W/m·K) to Cu-ETP / C11000 electrolytic tough-pitch copper (κ 386 W/m·K, ρ 8940 kg/m³, cp 385 J/kg·K — new copper_etp library entry, CDA/ASM handbook typical values for annealed material). Every other input — geometry, mesh (73 960 cells, identical to the baseline), boundary conditions, contact resistances, 30 W load, and solver settings — is unchanged.

Result: the material swap makes essentially no difference. At t = 9600 s (40 km), jetson_module reaches 71.334 °C and interface_plate reaches 72.115 °C, against the OFHC baseline's own solved 71.274 °C and 72.055 °C — a delta of +0.06 K, i.e. ETP copper runs marginally hotter, consistent with its ~1% lower conductivity. The two copper straps themselves are 47.6 °C / 47.2 °C (ETP) vs 47.55 °C / 47.15 °C (OFHC), also +0.06 K. Radiator temperatures are unchanged to three significant figures (19.41 °C / 19.85 °C, vs 19.41 °C / 19.85 °C baseline — differences under 0.01 K). Total radiative rejection at t = 9600 s is 24.21 W against 30.1 W injected (baseline: 24.2 W / 30.1 W) — the ~6 W shortfall is still going into thermal mass, i.e. the assembly has not reached steady state at either material choice, matching the baseline's own finding.

This ~0.06 K delta is consistent with the die-to-radiator path being dominated by the bolted-joint contact resistances (1.58 K/W per joint, ≫ the strap's own conduction resistance) rather than the strap material itself — see §3.3 and the discussion below. For this hardware, strap copper grade (OFHC vs Cu-ETP) is not a meaningful lever on peak payload temperature; joint quality/torque and the die-side TCR (0.10 K/W PTM7950 interface) dominate instead. All assumptions carried over from the baseline apply unchanged: constant 4.1667 m/s assumed ascent rate, altitude_environment (US76) BC from ground level, adiabatic enclosure, no mesh independence study, and the known CAD sliver-face checkMesh warning (solved via disable_fpe_trap, not a mesh fix).

9. Validation Methodology

  • Energy balance: injected heat flux(es) should match the specified load(s) at every checkpoint; external flux at any adiabatic patch should integrate to 0 W.
  • Interface balance: each coupled component-to-component interface should agree between the two sides reporting it, within a few percent.
  • Restart continuity (transient studies only): no temperature discontinuity at a segment restart boundary greater than ~1 K.
  • Physical plausibility: no region colder than the coldest applicable sink temperature or implausibly hot given the load and materials.
  • Not performed unless noted above: mesh independence; benchmark against measured hardware data.
  • Energy balance: jetson_heat_source = 30.1 W throughout (matches the specified 30 W load); every styrofoam_box-adjacent and other component-wall external patch integrates to 0.0 W (confirmed adiabatic).
  • Interface balance: every coupled interface in cht_kpis.json's interfaces_W agrees between its two reporting sides to within rounding (e.g. copper_strap_left_to_radiator_bracket_left −13.34 W vs radiator_bracket_left_to_copper_strap_left +13.34 W).
  • Restart continuity: checked across all 8 segment boundaries via transient_T_history.csv; no discontinuity found in log.chtMultiRegionFoam's Time = sequence at any of 1200/2400/…/8400 s.
  • Restart continuity vs. the source study: not applicable — this study was solved from t = 0 with a fresh 20 °C initial condition (the baseline's manifest/mesh were copied, not its solved field state), so there is no cross-study join to verify, only the end-of-run comparison this report is built around.
  • Physical plausibility: coldest region (radiator_plate_2, −3.5 °C min) is well above the ~−65 °C sky-sink floor for this altitude range; hottest region (interface_plate, 72.1 °C max) is well under any plausible hardware limit.
  • Not performed: mesh independence (inherited limitation from the baseline study, not re-assessed here since the mesh itself is unchanged).

10. Conclusions & Recommendations

  1. Cu-ETP is a viable substitute for OFHC copper in this strap application. The measured peak-temperature penalty (+0.06 K at jetson_module/interface_plate) is two orders of magnitude smaller than the ~50 K die-to-radiator ΔT this hardware runs at, and far smaller than the uncertainty already present in the bolted-joint TCR estimates (1.58 K/W, "total joint budget" — not independently measured for this hardware). If Cu-ETP offers a cost, availability, or manufacturability advantage over OFHC for this strap geometry, this analysis provides no thermal reason to prefer OFHC.
  2. The die-to-radiator path is joint-resistance-dominated, not strap-conduction- dominated. Of the four thermal contact resistances in the path (0.10 K/W at the die, 1.58 K/W ×2 in parallel between the cold plate and each strap end, effectively another ~1.58 K/W combined at the connector-block/bracket ends), the copper strap's own conduction resistance is a small fraction of the total — which is exactly why a 1% conductivity change between OFHC and Cu-ETP barely registers. Future thermal margin work on this hardware should prioritise the bolted-joint torque/interface quality over strap material selection.
  3. The assembly has not reached steady state by t = 9600 s at either material choice (24.2 W rejected vs 30.1 W injected, ~6 W still charging thermal mass) — this is a property of the ascent profile and thermal mass, unrelated to the material swap; see the companion transient-0-40km-dwell-60min study for what happens if the balloon holds at 40 km rather than continuing to ascend.
  4. No further action is needed on the strap material question for this hardware configuration; re-run only if the joint TCR estimates are replaced with measured values, since that is now the dominant uncertainty in the die-to-radiator path.
