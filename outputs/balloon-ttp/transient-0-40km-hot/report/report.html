Stratospheric Payload Transient Thermal Analysis: 0-40 km, 160 min, 30 W, Sun-Facing Radiator

Study: balloon-ttp/transient-0-40km-hot · Date: 2026-08-05 · Prepared by: agent-cfd-fea-engineer

1. Executive Summary

This is the sun-facing companion to the cold/no-sun study balloon-ttp/transient-0-40km: the identical 30 W Jetson payload, hardware and 0→40 km / 9600 s (160 min) ascent, but with radiator_plate exposed to direct solar + Earth-albedo flux (107.74 W/m², white-paint α_solar = 0.2, held constant for the whole ascent) while its twin radiator_plate_2 stays shaded, as in the cold case.

At t = 9600 s (40 km), the die (jetson_module) reached 78.2 °C and the cold plate (interface_plate) reached 79.2 °C — both ≈7 °C hotter than the cold case's 71.3 °C / 72.1 °C at the same instant, and both still rising, not yet at steady state. The sun-facing radiator (radiator_plate) ended the run at up to 31.8 °C, visibly warmer than its shaded twin's 22.8 °C peak — the intended asymmetry is present and of the expected sign (a radiator that's absorbing solar flux rejects less net heat for the same surface temperature). Both radiators together were still rejecting a comparable total to the cold case (≈23.1 W vs ≈24.2 W at t = 9600 s) against the same 30.1 W injected, so the ≈7 °C penalty comes from each radiator needing a higher surface temperature to reject the same net heat once part of its budget is offset by absorbed sun, not from a collapse in total rejection capability.

The 30 W load, adiabatic enclosure and all thermal contact resistances are unchanged from the cold case; see that study's report for the shared assumptions (no supplied flight profile, ascent rate, mesh defect, etc.). The one new assumption specific to this run: absorbed_flux_W_m2 is a single value computed at the 2500 m seed altitude and held fixed for the whole ascent (Earth's view factor to the radiator changes only ~8% over 0–40 km, and this ignores balloon attitude/self-shadowing dynamics — this is a worst-case, continuously-sun-normal scoping number, not a flight-review one).

All eight restart segments completed without a FOAM FATAL error or a restart discontinuity greater than 0.31 K (gate: < 1 K), and the injected heat flux held at 30.0–30.1 W throughout — see Section 9.

9. Validation Methodology

  • Energy balance: injected heat flux(es) should match the specified load(s) at every checkpoint; external flux at any adiabatic patch should integrate to 0 W.
  • Interface balance: each coupled component-to-component interface should agree between the two sides reporting it, within a few percent.
  • Restart continuity (transient studies only): no temperature discontinuity at a segment restart boundary greater than ~1 K.
  • Physical plausibility: no region colder than the coldest applicable sink temperature or implausibly hot given the load and materials.
  • Not performed unless noted above: mesh independence; benchmark against measured hardware data.
  • Confirmed against this study's cht_kpis.json and logs: jetson_heat_source held at 30.0–30.1 W throughout; foam_box_outer integrated to 0.0 W at every checkpoint; no restart discontinuity exceeded 0.31 K across any of the 7 segment boundaries (gate < 1 K); no region colder than the applicable sky sink or hotter than ~200 °C (observed range: −1.7 °C to 79.2 °C); coupled interfaces agree to within ≈10% or better between the two sides reporting them, consistent with the cold case's own ≈7% imbalance and not a new issue introduced by this BC change.
  • Study-specific limitation: absorbed_flux_W_m2 is a single fixed value from the 2500 m seed altitude, held constant for the whole ascent, and assumes the radiator stays continuously sun-normal — a worst-case scoping assumption, not a flight attitude model.

10. Conclusions & Recommendations

  • Sun exposure on one radiator adds ≈7 °C to the peak die/cold-plate temperature by the time the payload reaches 40 km (78.2 °C / 79.2 °C vs the cold case's 71.3 °C / 72.1 °C) — this is the direct answer to "does sun exposure matter for this design," and it does, though the assembly is still well short of typical Jetson operating limits at either result (confirm against the specific hardware variant's datasheet before treating either number as pass/fail).
  • The sun-facing/shaded radiator split (31.8 °C vs 22.8 °C peak) behaves exactly as intended: the two radiators are performing asymmetrically because one is absorbing solar+albedo flux that the other isn't, not because of any BC or solver defect.
  • Both cases still have combined radiator rejection in the same ballpark (≈23–24 W against ≈30 W injected) — the die-temperature penalty comes from each radiator needing a higher surface temperature to reject its share of the load once solar flux is subtracted from its net radiative budget, not from a rejection-capacity collapse.
  • Recommended next steps: (1) if the real mission has a known worst-case sun exposure duration/attitude, replace the constant-flux assumption here with a proper orbital/attitude-average, since this run is intentionally the continuously-sun-normal worst case; (2) confirm the Jetson hardware's actual thermal limit to convert either the cold- or hot-case peak into a real pass/fail margin; (3) if both radiators could plausibly be sun-facing simultaneously at some point in the flight (e.g. a slow roll), that case is not covered by either study run so far and would be more conservative than this one.
