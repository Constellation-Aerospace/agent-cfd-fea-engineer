Stratospheric Payload Thermal Analysis: 60-Minute Dwell at 40 km, 30 W

Study: balloon-ttp/transient-0-40km-dwell-60min · Date: 2026-08-05 · Prepared by: agent-cfd-fea-engineer

1. Executive Summary

This study picks up exactly where the cold-case ascent (balloon-ttp/transient-0-40km) left off — the same 30 W Jetson payload and hardware, starting from that study's actual solved thermal state at t = 9600 s (40 km reached), then held at a fixed 40 000 m altitude for another 3600 s (60 min) to see what happens once the balloon stops climbing.

The assembly is still heating up, not at steady state. The die (jetson_module) and cold plate (interface_plate) rose from 71.3 °C / 72.1 °C at the start of the dwell to 78.2 °C / 78.9 °C by t = 13200 s — a further +6.9 °C / +6.8 °C over the 60-minute hold, with no sign of turning over. The rate of rise is decelerating (2.25 °C per 1000 s in the first ~10 minutes of the dwell, down to 1.51 °C per 1000 s in the last ~10 minutes — a ~33% slowdown) but was still clearly positive at the end of the run. Total radiator rejection climbed from ≈24.2 W to ≈26.3 W against the constant 30.1 W injected, closing roughly a third of the gap between rejection and injection that was open at t = 9600 s, consistent with the assembly approaching — but not yet reaching — thermal equilibrium at this altitude.

The join between the two studies checks out cleanly: this study's first sample (t = 9620 s, jetson_module = 71.319 °C) matches the ascent study's own final reported value (71.274 °C at t = 9600 s) to within 0.05 °C, confirming the solved-state carryover in C1 worked correctly rather than silently restarting from a fresh initial condition.

Enclosure (styrofoam_box) stayed at the same isolated 20.0 °C it held throughout the ascent (see that study's report for why — it has no coupled thermal path to the rest of the assembly). All boundary conditions, materials and thermal contact resistances are identical to the ascent study; only altitude (now fixed) and elapsed time changed.

9. Validation Methodology

  • Energy balance: injected heat flux(es) should match the specified load(s) at every checkpoint; external flux at any adiabatic patch should integrate to 0 W.
  • Interface balance: each coupled component-to-component interface should agree between the two sides reporting it, within a few percent.
  • Restart continuity (transient studies only): no temperature discontinuity at a segment restart boundary greater than ~1 K.
  • Physical plausibility: no region colder than the coldest applicable sink temperature or implausibly hot given the load and materials.
  • Not performed unless noted above: mesh independence; benchmark against measured hardware data.
  • Confirmed against this study's cht_kpis.json and logs: jetson_heat_source held at 30.0–30.1 W throughout; foam_box_outer integrated to 0.0 W at every checkpoint; no restart discontinuity exceeded 0.083 K across either of the 2 internal segment boundaries (gate < 1 K); no region colder than the 40 km sky sink or hotter than ~200 °C (observed range: 0.3 °C to 78.9 °C); coupled interfaces agree to within ≈4% between the two sides reporting them — tighter than the ascent study's ≈7%, consistent with the system's rate of change slowing down.
  • Join to the source study confirmed: this study's first sample (t = 9620 s, jetson_module = 71.319 °C) matches the ascent study's own final reported value (71.274 °C at t = 9600 s) to within 0.05 °C — the C1 solved-state carryover worked as intended, this is not a fresh 20 °C restart mislabelled as a continuation.
  • Study-specific limitation: assumes the balloon holds exactly at 40 000 m for the full 60 minutes with no further ascent, descent or drift — does not apply once altitude starts changing again in a real flight.

10. Conclusions & Recommendations

  • The assembly had not reached thermal steady state after 60 more minutes at 40 km. Die/cold-plate temperature was still climbing at t = 13200 s (78.2 °C / 78.9 °C, up from 71.3 °C / 72.1 °C at the start of the dwell), though the rate of climb had slowed by roughly a third (2.25 → 1.51 °C per 1000 s) as radiator rejection (≈24.2 → ≈26.3 W) closed part of the gap to the constant 30.1 W load.
  • Extrapolating the decelerating trend, the assembly would need considerably longer than 60 minutes at a fixed 40 km to actually plateau — this run establishes the direction and rate of approach, not the eventual equilibrium temperature. If the true equilibrium value matters for a design decision, re-run C7 for a longer dwell (e.g. 3–4 hours) rather than extrapolating from this slope.
  • The 30 W load, adiabatic enclosure and thermal contact resistances continue to behave exactly as in the ascent study — this dwell didn't surface any new physical behaviour, just a continuation of the same charging trend at a slower, decelerating rate now that convection has essentially bottomed out at this altitude.
  • Recommended next steps: (1) if a real mission holds at altitude for longer than 60 minutes, re-run this study's C7 for that actual duration to find whether/when it plateaus; (2) combine this dwell mechanic with the sun-facing hot case (balloon-ttp/transient-0-40km-hot) if a real profile includes both climbing to 40 km and holding there in daylight — that combination hasn't been run yet; (3) confirm the Jetson hardware's actual thermal limit — this study alone cannot say whether 78–79 °C, or wherever a longer dwell would settle, is within margin.
