Stratospheric Payload Transient Thermal Analysis: 0-40 km, 160 min, 30 W

Study: balloon-ttp/transient-0-40km · Date: 2026-08-04 · Prepared by: agent-cfd-fea-engineer

1. Executive Summary

This study simulates a 30 W Jetson payload (die/module → cold plate → twin OFHC copper thermal straps → twin white-painted Al 6061 radiators, all inside an adiabatic EPS foam enclosure) through a stratospheric balloon ascent from 0 to 40 km over 9600 s (160 min), at a constant assumed ascent rate of 4.1667 m/s (250 m/min — no flight profile document was supplied for this study). Conduction, natural convection (Churchill correlation) and grey-body radiation to the US76 sky sink were solved with chtMultiRegionFoam, restarted across 8 segments as the ambient state was re-evaluated every 5000 m of altitude.

At the end of the simulated ascent (t = 9600 s, 40 km), the payload die (jetson_module) reached 71.3 °C and the cold plate (interface_plate) reached 72.1 °C, both still rising at a shallow, decelerating rate — the assembly has not reached thermal steady state by 40 km, consistent with the large thermal mass of the aluminum/copper/EPS assembly and the fixed 160-minute ascent window. Peak temperatures occurred at the final time step, not partway through the ascent. No thermal-limit number for this specific Jetson hardware variant was supplied with this task, so margin to a shutdown/derating threshold cannot be stated numerically here — compare 71–72 °C against the module's datasheet operating limit before treating this as pass/fail.

The twin radiators ended the run at a mean surface temperature of roughly 8 °C (range −3.4 to +19.9 °C across the two plates) and were jointly rejecting ≈24.2 W of radiated + convected heat to the sky sink at t = 9600 s, against 30.1 W injected at the cold plate — the ≈6 W shortfall is going into sensible heating of the assembly's thermal mass (consistent with temperatures still rising, not a modelling error; see Section 6 energy balance). Convective heat transfer collapsed by roughly an order of magnitude over the ascent (Churchill h ≈ 3.8 W/m²K at 2.5 km down to ≈0.6 W/m²K at 37.5 km) as expected with falling air density, while the radiative term stayed roughly constant — by 40 km, radiation is the dominant rejection path, exactly as intended by the radiator design. The enclosure (styrofoam_box) stayed at a uniform 20.0 °C throughout, confirming the adiabatic boundary condition is being honoured and 100% of the 30 W is routed through the strap/radiator path as intended.

All eight restart segments completed without a FOAM FATAL error or a temperature discontinuity greater than 0.26 K at any segment boundary (gate: < 1 K), and the injected heat flux held at 30.0–30.1 W throughout — see Section 9 for the full verification-gate results.

2. Problem Statement

Transient conjugate heat transfer analysis of a 30 W stratospheric-balloon payload: solid conduction through an aluminum cold plate, copper thermal straps and aluminum radiator brackets, coupled to natural convection and grey-body radiation at the two external radiator surfaces, with the enclosure walls modelled adiabatic. The assembly ascends from ground level to 40 km over 160 minutes; the ambient air temperature, pressure, density and sky-sink temperature are re-evaluated from the 1976 US Standard Atmosphere at 8 discrete altitudes (every 5000 m / 1200 s) and used to update the radiator boundary conditions between solver restarts.

6.2 Heat Balance

At t = 9600 s: jetson_heat_source injects 30.1 W; the two radiators jointly reject ≈24.2 W (12.09 + 12.12 W) by combined convection + radiation; foam_box_outer integrates to 0.0 W (adiabatic, confirmed). The ≈6 W gap is sensible heating of the assembly's thermal mass — temperatures are still rising at end-of-run, so the system has not reached steady state within the 160-minute ascent window.

6.3 Convective Collapse with Altitude

The Churchill-correlation convective film coefficient on the radiators fell roughly an order of magnitude over the ascent as air density dropped:

Stage Mid-altitude h_conv [W/m²K]
1 2 500 m 3.83 (seed)
5 22 500 m 1.32
6 27 500 m 0.98
7 32 500 m 0.75
8 37 500 m 0.60

The radiative film term stayed roughly constant (≈2.7–2.8 W/m²K) across the same range, so by 40 km radiation is the dominant rejection path, as the radiator design intends.

9. Validation Methodology

  • Restart continuity: no temperature discontinuity greater than 0.26 K observed at any of the 7 segment restart boundaries (gate: < 1 K) — confirms state was carried forward correctly across all 8 solver restarts.
  • Energy balance: jetson_heat_source held at 30.0–30.1 W throughout (target 30 W); foam_box_outer integrated to 0.0 W at every checkpoint (adiabatic BC honoured).
  • Interface balance: each coupled component-to-component interface agrees to within ≈7% or better between the two sides reporting it (see cht_kpis.json).
  • Physical plausibility: no region reported colder than the coldest sky sink (~−65 °C at 37 500 m) or implausibly hot; convective film coefficient collapse with altitude matches the expected Churchill-correlation trend (Section 6.3).
  • Cross-checked inputs: the US76 atmosphere table used to drive the boundary conditions was cross-checked against an independent published source (pdas.com digitisation of the NOAA/NASA/USAF tables) to within 2 K / a few % across 0–40 km before being used to drive 9600 s of solver time.
  • Not performed for this study: mesh independence (single mesh, factor 1.0); benchmark against measured flight thermal data (none available for this hardware).
  • Limitations: ascent rate is an assumption (4.1667 m/s constant, no flight profile document supplied — restage Section 7's boundary conditions against a real profile if one becomes available); altitude_environment/US76 sky-sink model is used from ground level, which is exact above ~20 km and conservative below it (neglects atmospheric IR, which would otherwise reduce the effective sky-sink term near sea level); solar/sun-facing case not run (this is the cold/no-sun case, absorbed_flux_W_m2 = 0).

10. Conclusions & Recommendations

  • The payload die and cold plate reached 71.3 °C / 72.1 °C at the end of the simulated 0→40 km, 160-minute ascent, still rising at end-of-run — the assembly has not settled to steady state within this flight duration. Compare against the specific Jetson hardware variant's datasheet operating limit to determine pass/fail margin; no such limit was supplied with this task.
  • 100% of the 30 W load is confirmed routed through the strap/radiator path (box adiabatic, 0.0 W leakage at every checkpoint) — the EPS enclosure is doing its intended job of thermal isolation.
  • Radiative rejection becomes the dominant heat-loss mechanism above the tropopause as convection collapses with air density; the twin-radiator design remains functional (non-zero, non-runaway rejection) through 40 km.
  • Recommended next steps: (1) obtain or confirm a real ascent/flight profile to replace the constant-rate assumption; (2) confirm the Jetson hardware's actual thermal limit to convert the 71–72 °C peak into a pass/fail margin; (3) if the mission includes sun-facing dwell time, re-run the hot case (Section on solar flux in the runbook) since this report covers only the cold/no-sun case; (4) run a mesh independence check if this configuration proceeds toward a design decision, since none was performed here.
