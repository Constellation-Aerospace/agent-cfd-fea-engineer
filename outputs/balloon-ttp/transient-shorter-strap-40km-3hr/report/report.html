Stratospheric Payload Thermal Analysis: Shorter-Strap Geometry, 3-Hour Mission (0-40 km Ascent + 60-min Dwell), Kolkata Sounding, Outer-Face-Only Radiation

Study: balloon-ttp/transient-shorter-strap-40km-3hr · Date: 2026-08-07 · Prepared by: agent-cfd-fea-engineer

1. Executive Summary

This study carries the payload's new shorter-strap geometry (Shorter straps.step) through a single continuous 3-hour mission — ascend 0→40 000 m over 2 h at a constant assumed 5.5556 m/s, then dwell at 40 000 m for 1 h, 30 W throughout — with two boundary- condition changes from every prior study in this project: ambient temperature below 30 km comes from a digitized real sounding (Kolkata, 12:00 UTC, 19 May 2026) instead of the US76 standard atmosphere, spliced seamlessly to US76 at 30 km (the two sources agree to ~0.3 K there); and each radiator plate radiates from its outer face only — the face touching the box interior is adiabatic, verified at every reported time to integrate to exactly 0.0 W (radiator_plate_top_inner/radiator_plate_bottom_inner external flux, Section 6.2).

At mission end (t = 10800 s, end of the dwell), interface_plate peaks at 135.5°C and jetson_module at 134.0°C — well above the ~78°C the equivalent-load, equivalent-duration transient-0-40km-dwell-60min study reached with the old whole-surface-radiating geometry. This is not a strap-length effect: restricting radiation to the outer face alone roughly halves each radiator's effective radiating area relative to every prior study (front+back both radiated before), so — following the T⁴ radiation law — the radiator, and everything upstream of it through the TTP path, must run substantially hotter to reject the same ~30 W through half the area. Heat balance at t = 10800 s: 30.11 W injected vs. 14.04 + 14.03 = 28.07 W rejected (93% — closer to balance than the old whole-surface design's 80% at its own dwell end), with the small residual still going into thermal mass: temperatures are still rising slowly at the end of the run (~1 K over the final 900 s segment), not yet at a true steady state, but close.

134°C at the jetson_module exceeds typical Jetson-class module junction/case temperature limits (commonly quoted around 85-100°C) — this is a genuine design flag, not just a number to report: the outer-face-only radiation requirement, at this radiator size and this altitude/load combination, does not keep the payload in a safe operating range for the full mission. A larger radiator area, a lower electrical load, or restoring some inner-face heat rejection (if the enclosure interior can tolerate it) would need to be evaluated to close this gap.

Key assumptions carried in this result: constant-rate ascent (unmeasured, chosen to land on clean segment boundaries); 30 W load unchanged from prior studies; the CAD's 3 unlabeled shortened-TTP-path solids modelled as bulk OFHC copper (a real CAD-export gap, not a modelling choice — see the runbook and manifest notes for the full reasoning); bolted-joint thermal contact resistances (1.58 K/W each, 0.1 K/W PTM7950 at the die) carried unchanged from every prior study in this project; enclosure modelled adiabatic (confirmed 0.0 W at styrofoam_box_outer throughout).

9. Validation Methodology

  • Energy balance: injected heat flux(es) should match the specified load(s) at every checkpoint; external flux at any adiabatic patch should integrate to 0 W.
  • Interface balance: each coupled component-to-component interface should agree between the two sides reporting it, within a few percent.
  • Restart continuity (transient studies only): no temperature discontinuity at a segment restart boundary greater than ~1 K.
  • Physical plausibility: no region colder than the coldest applicable sink temperature or implausibly hot given the load and materials.

  • Not performed unless noted above: mesh independence; benchmark against measured hardware data. Confirmed against this study's actual cht_kpis.json and segment logs:

  • Energy balance: jetson_heat_source = 30.11 W at t = 10800 s (target 30 W); styrofoam_box_outer = 0.0 W at every reported time (adiabatic enclosure, as assumed).

  • Outer-only radiation (the actual point of this study's BC change): radiator_plate_top_inner/radiator_plate_bottom_inner = 0.0 W at t = 10800 s (and at every checkpoint inspected) — confirms the inner faces radiate and convect nothing, only ..._outer (−14.04 / −14.03 W) rejects heat. radiator_plate_top_wall/radiator_plate_bottom_wall (the plate edges) = 0.0 W too.
  • Interface balance: every coupled interface agrees between its two reporting sides to within ~0.3% (e.g. strap_bottom_to_connector_bottom 14.4 W vs. connector_bottom_to_strap_bottom −14.44 W).
  • Restart continuity: all 12 segments (0→10800 s, 900 s each) completed with completed: true at their target end time and the correct step count (450 steps of deltaT=2.0 s per segment); each segment's solved field carried into the next via startFrom latestTime with no discontinuity. (This required fixing write_interval_s from 120 s, inherited from prior studies' 1200 s segments, to 90 s so it divides this study's 900 s segments evenly — see the manifest notes and the runbook's updated troubleshooting table; the original 120 s setting left each segment boundary with only a partial, diagnostic-only field write, which would have silently broken every restart from segment 2 onward.)
  • Physical plausibility: no region colder than the coldest ambient the mission sees (~−79°C, digitized tropopause minimum near 17 km) or implausibly hot for the materials involved — but see the Executive Summary's flag on jetson_module exceeding typical hardware temperature limits.
  • Not performed: mesh independence (consistent with every other transient study in this project to date); benchmark against measured hardware data.

10. Conclusions & Recommendations

  1. The outer-face-only radiation requirement is satisfied exactly — radiator_plate_top_inner/radiator_plate_bottom_inner external heat flux is 0.0 W at every checkpoint, confirmed by direct KPI extraction, not just by BC construction.
  2. Peak temperatures (135.5°C interface_plate, 134.0°C jetson_module) exceed typical Jetson-class module limits. This is the headline engineering finding: halving the radiator's effective area (by excluding the inner face) at this radiator size and 30 W load does not keep the payload in a safe range over a 40 km/3-hour mission, even though the shorter-strap TTP path itself is not the bottleneck (the bolted-joint TCRs dominate the die-to-radiator resistance, as found for strap material choice in the prior transient-0-40km-etp-copper comparison, and that conclusion carries forward here).
  3. Not yet at steady state at t = 10800 s — still rising ~1 K over the final 900 s segment, with a 2.04 W shortfall (30.11 W in vs. 28.07 W out) still going into thermal mass. A longer dwell would settle closer to a true peak, which would be higher than the 134-136°C reported here.
  4. Recommend evaluating a larger radiator area, a reduced electrical load, or a controlled inner-face heat path (if the enclosure interior can tolerate the resulting temperature rise) before treating this geometry/BC combination as flight-ready. The shorter strap itself is not implicated — its own conduction resistance was already shown thermally negligible next to the bolted joints.
  5. Every assumption in the Executive Summary (ascent rate, unlabeled-solid material assignment, TCR mapping) should be revisited against real flight/hardware data before this result is used for a go/no-go decision.
